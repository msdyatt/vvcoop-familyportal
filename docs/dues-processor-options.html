Collecting dues online: what it costs, and which processor to use

Three candidates, priced against the co-op's real dues structure of $300 per family plus $150 per child. No code has been written for any of them.

The bottom line

Use Stripe, and make bank transfer (ACH) the default way families pay. The processor choice matters less than the payment method: moving families from card to ACH saves roughly two-thirds of the fees, and it is the single biggest lever available. Stripe also has the only API good enough to keep a live dues ledger inside the portal.

What the numbers assume

I don't have the co-op's actual family count, so every figure below models 40 families averaging $600 each — one family fee of $300 plus two children at $150 — for $24,000 collected per year. Send me the real roster size and I'll restate the table; the shape of the answer doesn't change, only the magnitude. A sensitivity check across roster sizes is at the end.

Finding one

The Stripe nonprofit rate probably doesn't apply to you

Stripe discounts verified 501(c)(3) organizations to 2.2% + 30¢, and it would be natural to assume the co-op qualifies. But the discount requires that more than 80% of payment volume be tax-deductible donations, and co-op dues are payment for services received, not a donation. On that reading the co-op pays the standard 2.9% + 30¢ on cards.

This is worth one email to the co-op's CPA before budgeting either way — it is a $168/year difference at the modelled volume, and they will know how the co-op's dues are actually characterised. I have priced the tables below at the standard rate, which is the conservative assumption.

Finding two

ACH is the lever, not the vendor

Card fees scale with the amount charged; ACH bank transfers don't, because Stripe caps them at $5. On a $600 dues payment that is $17.70 by card against $4.80 by bank transfer — the card fee is nearly four times higher, and the gap widens with every child a family enrols.

Practically: make ACH the pre-selected option at checkout, keep card available for families who want it, and the default quietly does the work. Some co-ops add a card surcharge instead; that is a board decision about optics, not a technical one, and defaulting to ACH captures most of the benefit without anyone feeling penalised.

Zeffy's zero is real but not free: the platform covers processing costs by prompting each payer to add a voluntary tip on top. For donations that is a fair trade. For mandatory dues it means asking families to tip on a bill they are already required to pay, and the prompt is not something the co-op can turn off. That is a hospitality problem more than a financial one, and it is why I have not recommended it despite the headline number.

What each would cost to build

Fees are only half the decision. The portal already has the pieces a payments integration needs — Supabase Edge Functions with an established auth pattern, and row-level security to keep one family's balance private from another — so the question is what each vendor lets us connect to.

Option API Build What families would see
Stripe Full read/write, reliable webhooks Around a day Pay from inside the portal; balance and payment history live on their own dashboard
Cheddar Up Limited; built for exports A few hours Sent out to a Cheddar Up page; the portal can't show who has paid without manual reconciliation
Zeffy Read-only, one webhook Half a day Sent out to a Zeffy form; the portal can mirror payments after the fact but can't initiate them

Only Stripe supports the thing that makes this worth building at all: a family signing in, seeing what they owe, and paying it without leaving the portal — with the ledger updating itself. The other two are payment pages the co-op links out to, which is a real option if the goal is simply to stop collecting cheques, but it leaves an administrator reconciling a spreadsheet every term.

What I need from you to build it

The actual family count and typical children per family. So the figures above stop being a model. This also decides whether a Cheddar Up subscription is worth its monthly cost.
Whether the co-op absorbs the fee or passes it on. Absorbing it means dues net roughly $4.80–$17.70 less per family. Passing it on means showing families two prices. Board decision, not a technical one.
Whether dues are one payment or instalments. Instalments multiply the fixed 30¢ per transaction and make ACH's $5 cap less advantageous, since each smaller payment sits below the cap anyway.
Confirmation from the CPA on the donation question. Decides whether Stripe's nonprofit rate is available. Worth asking before the board commits to a budget line.
